Every park with a known location, clustered by region. Click a pin for the vertical, a link to the resort, and a jump down to its full card. Planning a drive? Press Plan a road trip, draw your route with a finger or mouse, and see the parks along it.

Pin positions are approximate — resort base area where known, town centre otherwise. Good enough to find a park, not survey-grade. Colour: orange = lift-served, green = opened 2022–26, teal = on Loam Pass.

Vertical drop is the one number every one of these parks reports, so it's the one that can be compared honestly across all of them. Trail mileage and lift counts are recorded inconsistently, so they're left on the individual cards rather than ranked here.

Vertical drop is hard to picture as a number. Stack a few parks side by side and the spread becomes obvious — the East Coast parks are not small, they're just next to Jackson Hole. Add as many as you like; peaks run west to east, and clicking one removes it.

Bluewood Bike Park (Under Construction)
Dayton, WA Coming 2027
LiftComing
▼
Descent~1,100 ft (top-to-bottom)
Launch Trails4 trails (green & blue), up to 6 by soft opening
Phase 1 Network20+ miles once complete
LiftNew high-speed quad replacing the 1979 Skyline Express triple
Trail BuilderCraig Piefer, Tamarack Trail Works
Est. OpeningFall 2027 preview; full ops summer 2028
Washington's third lift-served bike park, in an area with few places to ride — the state's other two lift parks (Snoqualmie, Stevens Pass) sit near Seattle, leaving eastern Washington, including the quarter-million-person Tri-Cities area, over two hours from lift-served DH. Four top-to-bottom trails (~1,100 ft descent, green/blue) are under construction with Craig Piefer of Tamarack Trail Works — Green Giant is already taking shape, with three more planned — building toward a 20+ mile network in phase one and 10+ trails long-term, plus a beginner pump track with its own conveyor lift at the base. It all depends on a new high-speed quad replacing the original 1979 Skyline Express triple; per Bluewood's August 2026 GM letter, shipping delays on lift components mean it won't be finished before the 2026-27 ski season, with installation now planned for after that season and completion targeted for September/early October 2027. Trails are expected to open for a preview that fall, with full lift-served operations (rentals, lessons) arriving summer 2028.
